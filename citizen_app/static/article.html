1. Modus Operandi Overview

Digital arrest scams represent an organized psychological extortion tactic where organized cyber syndicates pose as senior law enforcement officials (e.g., Central Bureau of Investigation, Enforcement Directorate, Narcotics Control Bureau, or Mumbai/Delhi Police Crime Branch).

The perpetrators establish communication through automated IVR calls claiming that the victim's parcel containing illegal narcotics or forged passports has been intercepted at international customs. Victims are then transferred to video calls featuring counterfeit police backdrops, badges, forged Supreme Court arrest warrants, and official emblems.

Core Fact: No law enforcement agency, judicial magistrate, or customs department has the legal provision to declare a citizen under "digital arrest" or conduct interrogations over Skype/WhatsApp.

2. Attack Architecture & Step-by-Step Execution

Analysis of multiple reported FIRs reveals a standardized four-phase extortion funnel:

01
Initial Contact & Shock Initiation:

The victim receives an automated call claiming a courier shipment or telephone line disconnection due to money laundering charges.

02
Isolation & Video Interrogation:

The scammer directs the victim to Skype or WhatsApp video, insisting they isolate themselves in a locked room under the threat of national security secrecy.

03
Fabricated Judicial Documents:

High-resolution forged letters bearing fake signatures of Supreme Court judges and RBI governors are transmitted to instill extreme panic.

04
Financial Depletion to Mule Accounts:

The victim is coerced to liquidate fixed deposits, mutual funds, or transfer savings to a designated "RBI Verification Escrow Account" which is immediately laundered through layer-1 and layer-2 mule networks.

4. Behavioral & Digital Red Flags

Citizens must remain vigilant against the following decisive indicators:

  • Demand for Continuous Video Surveillance: Insisting you keep your camera on while traveling to the bank or staying isolated at home.
  • Imposition of "National Secrecy": Threatening prosecution under Official Secrets Act if you consult family members or legal counsel.
  • Payment to Private Account Numbers: Legitimate government fees and deposits are never collected through individual savings or current accounts.
  • Urgent Deadlines: Artificial time constraints preventing verification through local police stations.

5. Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for Victims

Immediate Golden Hour Action Protocol:

  1. Disconnect Communication: Immediately terminate the video call. Block the contact number.
  2. Dial 1930 Immediately: Contact the National Cyber Crime Helpline within 2 hours to initiate transaction stoppage across the Indian banking network.
  3. Notify Your Bank: Instruct your bank branch manager to freeze the originating account to prevent further unauthorized debits.
  4. File Formal NCRP Report: Register the detailed complaint with transaction UTR numbers on this portal.

7. Evidence Preservation Checklist

When reporting the matter to the Cyber Crime Police Station, ensure you preserve the following technical artifacts:

  • High-resolution screenshots of the video call and display avatars.
  • Call logs showing exact timestamps and incoming phone numbers.
  • Bank account statements showing the beneficiary account number, IFSC code, and 12-digit UTR reference number.
  • Copies of forged documents, warrants, or identity cards transmitted by scammers.
