Common Cyber Frauds: How Scammers Trap Victims

Understand the exact modus operandi, warning signs, and real case patterns before scammers target your hard-earned money.

4 min read

Digital Arrest & Fake Police Video Calls

Fraudsters pose as CBI, ED, or State Police officers over WhatsApp/Skype video calls using fake police uniforms and forged supreme court warrants to intimidate victims into transferring security deposits.

Key Red Flags:
  • Demands to stay locked in a room on continuous video call
  • Threats of immediate arrest without physical summons
  • Asking you to transfer money to a "RBI clearance account"
Official Clarification:

Law Enforcement Agencies and Judicial Courts NEVER conduct arrests or demand money via video calls.

Electricity Bill Update Phishing APKs

Victims receive urgent SMS claiming their electricity will be disconnected at 9:30 PM due to unpaid bills. When the victim calls the given helpline, they are tricked into installing a malicious APK that steals NetBanking OTPs.

Key Red Flags:
  • SMS from personal 10-digit mobile numbers instead of official sender IDs
  • Instructions to install third-party APK files from WhatsApp links
  • Asking for ₹10 recharge to "update meter software"
Official Clarification:

Electricity boards never send disconnection notices via personal WhatsApp or ask for third-party APK installations.

Part-Time Telegram Job & Crypto Tasks

Promising ₹3,000–₹5,000 daily for liking YouTube videos or reviewing Google maps. After paying small initial profits, victims are coerced into "prepaid VIP crypto tasks" costing lakhs of rupees.

Key Red Flags:
  • Unsolicited job offers on WhatsApp from international country codes
  • Being added to Telegram investment groups showing fake profit screenshots
  • Demands for deposits to "unlock accumulated commission"
Official Clarification:

No legitimate company hires via Telegram or demands money from candidates to complete daily tasks.

Fake Customer Care & Remote Screen Sharing

Fraudsters place fake customer support numbers on Google Search for banks, airlines, or courier services. When contacted, they instruct victims to install AnyDesk/RustDesk to steal login credentials.

Key Red Flags:
  • Caller asking you to download screen sharing apps (AnyDesk, QuickSupport)
  • Asking you to enter your UPI PIN to "receive a refund"
  • Asking for the 9-digit remote connection code
Official Clarification:

Entering UPI PIN is ONLY required to deduct money from your account, never to receive refunds.

Cyber Hygiene: Essential Dos and Don'ts

Follow these guidelines recommended by the Ministry of Home Affairs to protect your digital identity.

DOs: Best Cyber Practices

  • Enable 2-Factor Authentication (2FA) on all email, bank, and social media accounts.
  • Verify Sender IDs: Official bank SMS always have alpha headers like VK-HDFCBK, AX-SBIINB.
  • Report Immediately: Call 1930 within the golden hour if you notice unauthorized debits.
  • Use Official App Stores: Only install applications from Google Play Store or Apple App Store.

DON'Ts: High Risk Actions

  • Never Share OTP or PIN: No bank employee or police officer will ever ask for your OTP.
  • Never Accept Remote Control: Do not install AnyDesk or TeamViewer at the instruction of an unknown caller.
  • Never Transfer Money for "Verification": Government agencies never require deposits to verify bank accounts.
  • Never Click Unknown Links: Avoid opening payment links sent via SMS claiming lottery wins or bill penalties.
